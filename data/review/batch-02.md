## Capital Restaurant — Gem 73
*Cantonese family restaurant, since about 1986 · Chinatown, San Francisco · Deep cut · midday, evening*

**Story.** Open since around 1986. ## The Connecticut Yankee — Gem 77
*1907 saloon turned Boston sports bar · Potrero Hill, San Francisco · Deep cut · midday, evening, late*

**Story.** Built in 1907 as Hilda's Saloon, reportedly from the lumber of a Red Cross earthquake shack. It was a boarding house, then a Prohibition bootlegging cellar, and in 1988 a man sold his baseball cards to buy it. Today it is Potrero Hill's home base for Boston fans.

**Order this.** The CY burger or the fried chicken sandwich, with salt-and-vinegar fries.

**Why this score.** One of the best backstories on the list, from a hand-built saloon to a Prohibition bootleg cellar. The food is good bar food, which keeps the signature mark modest.

## Yasukochi's Sweet Stop — Gem 85
*Coffee crunch cake counter, since 1974 · Japantown, San Francisco · Deep cut · morning, midday*

**Story.** Thom Yasukochi learned the coffee crunch cake from Antoine, the candy maker at Blum's, the long-gone San Francisco chain that invented it. He opened this counter inside a Japantown market in 1974 to keep it alive, and a Blum's daughter later said it tasted exactly right. His grandson Kenji Yick now makes the temperamental candy topping.

**Order this.** A slice of the coffee crunch cake. Go early.

**Why this score.** The last keeper of a lost San Francisco dessert, still made by the founders' grandson. Atmosphere is just a glass case in the corner of a market, which holds that mark down.

## Tamales Acapulco — Gem 82
*Guerrero-style tamale trailer · Fruitvale, Oakland · Deep cut · morning, midday*

**Story.** Teresa Mondragon, known to everyone as Doña Tere, sells tamales from a trailer in a Fruitvale market parking lot. She was part of the group of immigrant vendors who pushed Oakland to change its street-food rules about two decades ago. Her red mole goes into the masa itself, Guerrero style, not just on top.

**Order this.** A tamal de mole rojo and a couple of chicharrón tacos de canasta. On a cold day, ask for champurrado.

**Why this score.** Regional Guerrero cooking from a vendor who helped change Oakland's street-food rules. The setting is a trailer in a market parking lot, which keeps atmosphere moderate.
