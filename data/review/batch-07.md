## Chong Qing Xiao Mian — Gem 54
*Chongqing noodle shop · Chinatown, San Francisco · Open secret · midday, evening*

**Story.** A small Kearny Street shop that sticks to regional Chinese noodles, with a handful of tight tables and a steady crowd. The dan dan noodles come in a nutty, rich broth loaded with wheat noodles and enough Sichuan pepper to leave a tingle, and wonton and spicy beef soups fill out the rest of the menu.

## Lung Fung Bakery — Gem 59
*Cantonese bakery · Central Richmond, San Francisco · Deep cut · morning, midday*

**Story.** A plain Clement Street bakery known for baked BBQ pork buns with crisp tops and enough filling to spill out the sides. They often come off the sheet still warm and go fast, so locals turn up at the 7:30 opening and buy by the dozen. Ham-and-corn and green onion buns are the backup.

**Order this.** Baked BBQ pork buns, a dozen; ham-and-corn bun if they're gone.

**Why this score.** The baked BBQ pork bun here is the Richmond's most-chased pastry and sells out in batches. Everything else is a plain neighborhood bakery counter.

## Tia Margarita — Gem 72
*Northern Mexican restaurant and bar, since 1963 · Central Richmond, San Francisco · Open secret · midday, evening*

**Story.** Opened in 1963 by two bartender couples in a bar with spare back rooms; chef Alejandro Espinosa wrote the Northern Mexican menu. Virginia Hobbs brought her daughters in after her partners died, and her granddaughter runs it now. The neon sign dates from opening day and the horseshoe bar from The Bostonian, the bar before it.

**Order this.** A fresh lime margarita at the horseshoe bar, then carne asada or chile rellenos.

**Why this score.** A 1963 family restaurant that kept its neon marquee, a horseshoe bar older than itself and a menu written by its first chef. Three generations of one family have run it.

**Practical.** Reservations taken · Full bar · Stayed open through a 1988 fire and the 1989 quake

## Lois the Pie Queen — Gem 71
*Soul food breakfast house, since 1951 · Bushrod, Oakland · Local legend · morning, midday*

**Story.** Lois Davis cooked for her father's church and, with her sister, catered up to 10,000 sandwiches a day for Black shipyard workers in the 1940s. She and her husband Roland opened in Berkeley in 1951 and moved to 60th and Adeline in the mid-1970s. Her son Chris runs it. The Reggie Jackson breakfast means two pork chops and two eggs.

**Order this.** The Reggie Jackson breakfast, then a slice of pie to go.

**Why this score.** Possibly California's oldest Black-owned restaurant, still in family hands, with walls of photos and a breakfast named for Reggie Jackson. The pies are the reason for the name.  
Uniqueness 74 · Atmosphere 82 · Story 94 · Signature item 80 · Under the radar 25

**Practical.** Breakfast-heavy menu · Walls lined with photos of Black American figures
