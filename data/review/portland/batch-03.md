## Las Brasas — Gem 69
*Costa Rican rotisserie · Downtown Gresham, Gresham · Deep cut · midday, evening*

**Story.** Jorge Zuniga started cooking chicken in 1982 at another restaurant named Las Brasas. He and his wife Zoraida opened Casa de Pollo down the block in 2005, later moved it to Lake Oswego, then took two years off. This Costa Rican rotisserie is the comeback, in a rebuilt former laundromat.

**Order this.** The marinated rotisserie chicken, which you can watch turning from the counter, as a casado plate with rice and beans. Ask for Zoraida's chimichurri.

**Why this score.** Costa Rican cooking is hard to find around here, and the owner has been on chicken duty since 1982, so uniqueness and story hold up. The room is a plain converted laundromat, it only opened in February 2026, and one local paper is all the press so far.

## Murata — Gem 62
*Old-school Japanese dining room · Downtown, Portland · Open secret · midday, evening*

**Story.** This Japanese restaurant in the 200 Market office building has been owned by one family since 1988, and it holds a sushi bar and three tatami rooms. Guidebooks have long noted its crowd of locals and Japanese businesspeople, and Portland critics still put it on their best-of lists.

**Order this.** The weekday teishoku lunch: pick a protein, anything from sashimi to fried chicken, and it comes with rice, pickles and cucumber salad. Call a few days ahead if you want a tatami room. At dinner, critics say skip the set menus for nigiri a la carte.

**Why this score.** Tatami rooms and a family owner since 1988 make it rarer than the average downtown Japanese spot, and the weekday lunch set keeps getting recommended. We found little story beyond the ownership date, and it is widely covered, so nobody would call it a secret.

## Goose Hollow Inn — Gem 72
*Family-run Reuben tavern · Goose Hollow, Portland · Local legend · midday, evening, late*

**Story.** Bud Clark bought Ann's Tavern at SW 19th and Jefferson in 1967 after the city razed his old bar, and named it to revive a lost neighborhood name. He went on to be mayor while his wife Sigrid ran the place. The Clark family still owns it, and the Reuben is the point.

**Order this.** The Reuben: corned beef and Reuben sauce, toasted on rye. Pair it with a pint and take it out to the deck under the birch trees.

**Why this score.** A tavern opened by a future mayor that gave the neighborhood back its name, still in his family, with a Reuben that is its whole reputation. It is on every list, and at heart it is a tavern with one famous sandwich, which keeps uniqueness out of the 90s.
